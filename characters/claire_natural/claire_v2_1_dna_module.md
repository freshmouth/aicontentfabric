# Claire Natural V2.1 DNA Module

Status: active creative gate for future Claire tests. Daily automation remains paused until this module is validated.

Source framework: `D:/Downloads/ugc-script-generation-framework.md`

## Purpose

Claire V2.1 is a stricter creative DNA layer for Claire Natural. It exists because the previous Claire output became too slow, polite, predictable, and visually passive. Future Claire videos must feel like a real supermarket discovery with emotional stakes, not a calm health lesson.

The north star:

> Claire catches a hidden supermarket trap in real time, proves it visually, and makes the viewer feel slightly betrayed by the package they trusted.

This module does not replace the pipeline. It controls concept, script, shot planning, hook captions, and QA before the existing generation pipeline is allowed to run.

## Non-Negotiable Framework

Every V2.1 script must follow the locked UGC structure exactly:

- 0:00-0:08 Hook: open psychological loop, no guide or product mention.
- 0:08-0:18 Problem: hidden cause the viewer has not connected.
- 0:18-0:28 Failed fix: why the obvious healthy choice keeps failing.
- 0:28-0:38 Mechanism: explain the scan/mechanism first, then introduce the guide if needed.
- 0:38-0:48 Honesty: realistic expectation, limitation, or timeline.
- 0:48-0:50 Soft close: no command, no ad voice, no hard CTA.

The backend guide is allowed only after the mechanism is clear. It must never appear before 0:22.

## Required Inputs

Do not generate from a broad topic alone. Each concept must define:

- `PRODUCT_NAME`: usually `40+ Label Scan guide`, unless a specific campaign overrides it.
- `PRODUCT_CATEGORY`: digital supermarket label-scan guide or specific product category.
- `CORE_MECHANISM`: the one concrete check that explains the video.
- `COMMON_PROBLEM`: what the viewer already feels or fears.
- `HIDDEN_CAUSE`: the package, ingredient, serving-size, sugar, oil, or marketing trick they missed.
- `FAILED_ALTERNATIVE`: the obvious healthy behavior that does not solve the problem.
- `HONEST_TIMELINE`: what changes realistically and when.
- `USAGE_RITUAL`: the simple behavior Claire wants installed.
- `TARGET_AVATAR`: women over 40 unless a campaign specifies otherwise.
- `AUDIENCE_TEMP`: cold, warm, or retargeting.
- `PLATFORM`: TikTok, Reels, or Shorts.
- `DM_ASSET`: the exact guide page, checklist, or image sent after the comment automation.

## Claire V2.1 Identity

Claire is no longer a soft kitchen educator. She is a fast-paced supermarket health investigator with a skeptical, protective energy. She is still warm and believable, but her role is to expose how ordinary packages manipulate health-conscious women.

She should feel like:

- She just found something on the label that annoyed her.
- She is trying to save the viewer from wasting money or being fooled.
- She is speaking quickly because the first three seconds matter.
- She is showing proof with her hands, not explaining abstractly.

She should not feel like:

- A wellness lecturer.
- A calm recipe host.
- A generic AI avatar.
- A beauty influencer holding products.
- A medical authority diagnosing disease.

## Emotional Engine

Every concept must choose one primary emotional driver:

- Betrayal: "the package trained you to trust the wrong thing."
- Anger at marketing: "you paid extra for a word on the front."
- Disgust: "look what is actually inside this healthy-looking choice."
- Regret avoidance: "this is the grocery habit I wish I caught earlier."
- Identity protection: "smart shoppers still get tricked by this."
- Relief: "this is not your fault; the label is designed this way."
- Urgency: "check this before it goes in your cart again."

By second 8, the viewer must know what is at risk emotionally.

## Hook Rules

The hook is a hard gate. If the hook fails, the whole concept fails.

Required:

- Visible object in frame within the first 0.5 seconds.
- One clear accusation, test, visual promise, or betrayal claim.
- Under 18 spoken words.
- Sentence-case overlay, not all caps.
- No product or guide mention.
- No slow setup.
- No "may", "might", "can help", "tips", "healthy swap", or soft wellness phrasing.

Good V2.1 hook patterns:

- "This protein bar is dessert wearing a gym badge."
- "You paid extra for the word protein."
- "I gave this label three seconds to confess."
- "This oatmeal trick works because the front is lying."
- "That healthy-looking granola is a cookie with better marketing."
- "The ingredient list tells a different story."

Weak hooks that fail:

- "This may surprise you."
- "Here are three healthy swaps."
- "If you want to eat healthier..."
- "Let me explain why this matters."
- "This could be affecting your hormones."

## Visual DNA

Every scene needs a visual job. Do not make a scene where Claire simply talks unless it is the honesty close-up.

Required visual actions:

- Flip the package to the label.
- Circle or tap the suspicious line.
- Dump a measured amount into a bowl or hand.
- Compare front label versus nutrition panel.
- Put the product back on the shelf.
- Show a cart decision.
- Show a real alternative next to the rejected product.
- Use a receipt, shelf tag, measuring spoon, or phone note as proof.

Allowed visual intensity:

- Dramatic supermarket aisle angle.
- Close product label shot.
- Awkward iPhone crop.
- Hand partially blocking frame.
- Fast rack between product and Claire's face.
- Real kitchen counter proof demonstration.
- Real product packaging from existing supermarket brands.

Banned:

- Fake Claire-branded products.
- Generic "brand free" packaging.
- Repeated orange shots unless the video is actually about oranges.
- Repeated front-facing selfie talking shots.
- Static kitchen chat.
- Grotesque medical visuals.
- Fake medical exams.
- Floating or sliding products.
- Objects inserted through jars, spoons, hands, or faces.
- Any visual that looks like three unrelated videos were scrambled together.

## Real Product Rule

If a video names or shows a product, use real supermarket products or realistic category packaging. Never use Claire as the brand. The package must support the concept and cannot be a random prop.

The video can criticize a category or label pattern without defaming a specific brand. Prefer "this type of protein granola" over medical or legal claims about one named brand.

## Script Voice

Claire's delivery should be fast, specific, and spoken for the ear.

Required:

- One idea per sentence.
- Short lines.
- Direct second-person stakes.
- Personal discovery framing when making health-adjacent claims.
- The viewer is absolved: "you are not lazy; the label is doing this."
- The mechanism is concrete: sugar, serving size, oil type, fiber, protein ratio, calories per real portion, or ingredient order.

Banned:

- Long paragraphs.
- Passive "wellness education" voice.
- Generic claims.
- "May", "might", "could help", "support", "promote", unless legally required in a specific context.
- Disease treatment, cure, detox, guaranteed fat loss, or guaranteed hormone claims.
- Hard CTA verbs in the script: shop, buy, click, link, grab, order, get yours.

## Caption And Overlay Rules

Hook caption style:

- Sentence case.
- White rounded rectangle.
- Black text.
- Tight lateral padding.
- Two lines max.
- No all caps unless a real quoted package word is shown.
- No distorted box shapes.
- The white fill must cover the full background behind multi-line text.

Subtitles:

- Native TikTok-style clean captions.
- Smaller than the hook caption.
- Timed after final audio, not before audio.
- No captions appearing before speech.

## Product Bridge

Claire can use the same backend offer across different topics, but every topic must map to one concrete page or deliverable inside the guide.

Example:

- Video topic: protein granola.
- Mechanism: front-label protein claim hides sugar and serving-size math.
- DM asset: protein snack label checklist page from the 40+ Label Scan guide.

The guide must feel like the natural next step, not a random ebook mention.

## QA Gates

A Claire V2.1 concept is approved only if all gates pass.

Framework gate:

- Product or guide absent before 0:22.
- Zero hard CTA verbs in spoken script.
- One honest limitation or timeline exists.
- Exactly one core mechanism is taught.
- Script can be spoken in 50 seconds.

Hook gate:

- Object appears immediately.
- The first line creates tension.
- No weak modal language.
- Overlay is sentence-case and readable.

Visual gate:

- Every scene has a distinct visual job.
- No repeated irrelevant object.
- No fake product branding.
- No generic static face-to-camera chain.
- First frame is a pattern interrupt.

Emotional gate:

- One primary emotion is named.
- Viewer has a reason to care by second 8.
- The script creates stakes without making illegal health promises.

Continuity gate:

- No repeated phrase at clip boundaries.
- No unfinished end sentence before the next clip.
- No abrupt self-interruption unless intentionally scripted.
- Segment audio must not duplicate the final word of the previous segment.

Boring detector:

- If the hook could be said in a school health class, fail it.
- If the first frame could be a generic wellness stock photo, fail it.
- If the concept can be summarized as "eat healthier", fail it.
